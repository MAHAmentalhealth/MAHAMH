MAHA INSTITUTE / MENTAL HEALTH

Mental health reform priorities.

These areas connect the Institute’s Mental Health and Overmedicalization Summit with the guide’s focus. They are questions for public action, not a claim that every linked organization endorses the same policy.

01 / CHOICE

Informed decisions and safer changes

People need clear information about benefits, harms, alternatives, and withdrawal when making treatment decisions.

02 / CHILDREN

Care beyond automatic medicalization

Children and families need careful assessment and support that considers their circumstances before medication becomes the default response.

03 / EVIDENCE

Transparent research and accountability

Open evidence and meaningful outcome measures help the public assess claims about treatment and policy.

04 / SUPPORT

More paths to care and connection

Policy can widen access to peer, community, and non-drug forms of support alongside existing care.

MAHA INSTITUTE / IN THE STATES

State councils connect advocates and policy.

MAHA Institute’s state councils bring selected local advocates and other leaders together to advance state-level priorities. Their policy work and relationships with state lawmakers can help make mental health reform durable beyond a single federal announcement. The councils are coordinated through the Institute; this is information about their role, not an open membership invitation.

BILL SPOTLIGHT / EXAMPLES

See how an issue moves.

These examples show different outcomes and questions worth following. They are selected for relevance, not an endorsement of every provision. The status below is a dated snapshot; always check the legislature’s record for the latest text and action.

TEXAS / HB 1151 / 2025

Parental choice and child welfare

Texas addressed when a parent’s refusal of psychiatric treatment or psychotropic medication may be treated as neglect, with exceptions where a child faces specified harm. The law took effect September 1, 2025.

Read the enrolled text ↗
FLORIDA / SB 560 / 2026

Medication oversight in foster care

A broader child welfare bill included changes to medical reports for psychotropic medication in state custody. It passed the Senate but died in House messages on March 13, 2026. Its path shows why the latest chamber action matters.

Follow the funding behind policy.

Federal funding notices can shape what states and local providers actually build. Future briefs can examine each opportunity’s eligibility, allowable activities, reporting requirements, and incentives, including whether it expands meaningful choice or reinforces a narrow response to distress.

There is no current funding recommendation in this guide. For an opportunity under consideration, read the original notice and state implementation documents before drawing conclusions.

A SIMPLE WAY TO FOLLOW AN ISSUE
01

Find the text

Search an issue or bill number in Delilah or Congress.gov. Read the actual bill and note its jurisdiction and date.

02

Watch the status

Bookmark a bill for updates, then confirm the latest action on the relevant legislature’s official page.

03

Separate rules from bills

Agency rulemaking has its own record and comment deadlines. Use Regulations.gov for federal dockets.
