01 / THE APPROACH

We need a wider view of mental health.

Social isolation, economic insecurity, environmental exposures, nutrition, technology, trauma, community disconnection, and the treatments intended to help may all play a role. The relative contribution of each remains unclear, and no single institution, profession, or political movement has all the answers.

Progress requires humility, curiosity, scientific rigor, and a willingness to ask difficult questions. It also requires protecting the ability of individuals, families, and clinicians to make informed decisions with true autonomy.

We can question orthodoxies, investigate evidence gaps, and explore new approaches without dismissing people who benefit from existing care. This effort does not seek to restrict access to any treatment or prescribe one path for everyone; it seeks meaningful choices, accurate information, and a broader range of support.

Our standard

We distinguish what is known, what is debated, and what remains uncertain. The guide aims to amplify and resource existing work rather than absorb it. We link to original sources and revisit resources as evidence changes. Editorial inclusion is separate from financial support; no listing is paid placement or blanket endorsement.

A NOTE ON MEDICATION CHANGES

Considering a psychiatric drug change?

Withdrawal can be difficult and abrupt changes may be dangerous. Start with information about the drug, preparation, and support; discuss decisions with a qualified prescriber who can consider your circumstances.
